Service Catalog research

Contributed to research planning, survey design and implementation, qualitative and quantitative analysis, and decision documentation to learn how students, staff, IT professionals, faculty, and researchers expect OIT services to be organized. The work included service categorization, naming, grouping, acronyms, tool naming, and umbrella categories.

Preferred and legal name-change experience

Supported usability testing and synthesis evaluating HR, Registrar, and OIT information. Findings highlighted the value of role-based headings and numbered steps, along with opportunities to make help, contact information, system impacts, timelines, and confirmation more visible and understandable.

Form best practices & style guidance

Created and iteratively refined accessible form guidance and a related design-system style guide. The documentation supports clear labels, form flow, validation, error messaging, privacy, consent, and accessible user-centered language across university services.

Contribution themes

Additional documented work spans usability-testing support, interview preparation, note synthesis, survey analysis, heuristic and comparative research, current-state captures, prototypes, information architecture, service catalog content, research summaries, and team handoff materials.
