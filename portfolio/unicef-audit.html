UX AUDIT · UX2 COURSE PROJECT

UNICEF.org

A semester-long UX audit evaluating how UNICEF.org builds clarity, trust, accessibility, and confidence for potential supporters and donors.

Role
UX researcher & auditor

Methods
Heuristic, accessibility, content, IA & journey evaluation

Deliverables
Audit report & stakeholder deck

Why UNICEF?

UNICEF’s global scale, diverse audience, and mission-driven context made it an ideal audit subject. When a website must communicate humanitarian impact and turn care into action, clarity and trust are not just design goals—they are responsibilities.

The audit evaluated how clearly the site communicates UNICEF’s mission, programs, and impact to potential donors and supporters, including people reading, signing up for updates, or completing a donation.

Scope & methods

I examined the primary, sub-, footer, and mobile navigation; homepage; “What We Do” page; donation page; and end-to-end donation task flow. The work was informed by the lens of a cautious, research-oriented first-time visitor with limited time.

  • Visual design evaluation
  • Nielsen heuristic evaluation
  • Accessibility evaluation
  • Content evaluation
  • Information-architecture evaluation using a closed card sort
  • User-journey evaluation of the donation flow

What was working

UNICEF’s storytelling, photography, consistent branding, and clear top-of-page hierarchy create a powerful first impression. Familiar form patterns, persistent labels, clear confirmation messaging, and predictable navigation provide useful foundations for the experience.

Key findings

Trust needs to arrive earlier

The homepage’s crisis-focused hero creates urgency but does not clearly introduce UNICEF, expand its acronym, or explain its broader work above the fold. The donation flow also redirects U.S. visitors to UNICEF USA without a bridging explanation, creating a major trust-risk moment.

Accessibility gaps affect navigation

Findings included insufficient contrast in key areas, skipped heading levels, inconsistent alt text, too many keyboard tab stops within visual cards, and missing visible focus indicators in the donation experience.

Decision-making needs more support

Users encounter limited progress feedback, late form validation, unclear monthly-giving consequences, and taxes/totals that arrive later than expected. The information architecture was generally intuitive, but “Who We Are” and “What We Do” felt overlapping and location content was ambiguous.

Priority recommendations

  1. Add a plain-language UNICEF descriptor and expand the acronym above the fold.
  2. Explain the transition to UNICEF USA before redirecting donors.
  3. Fix WCAG AA contrast failures and sequential heading structure.
  4. Strengthen the Donate call to action and add real-time validation.
  5. Add donation-flow progress, clear monthly-giving disclosure, useful alt text, and visible focus indicators.
  6. Clarify overlapping navigation categories and surface help resources more prominently.

Reflection

The audit showed a gap between emotional trust and decision confidence: the site is compelling for people already motivated by UNICEF’s work, but first-time visitors need clearer context, transparent donation information, and more accessible interaction patterns. Many of the highest-impact recommendations are targeted, low-effort changes that could strengthen trust, accessibility, and conversion.
