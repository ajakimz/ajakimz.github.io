UX REDESIGN · COURSE PROJECT

Cheyenne Mountain Zoo

Redesigning the online ticket-purchasing flow so guests can plan a visit with less friction and more confidence.

Role
Lead UX researcher & designer

Scope
Research, wireframes, prototype & testing

Tools
Figma & usability testing

Project overview

This project focused on the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo’s online ticket-purchasing experience. The existing process created confusion, unnecessary steps, and cognitive load. I designed a streamlined high-fidelity prototype that reduces redirects, clarifies pricing and totals, and helps guests complete checkout with confidence.

The challenge

How might we simplify and clarify the ticket-buying journey so visitors can complete a guest checkout for two adult tickets in fewer steps and with greater confidence?

The design needed to work within an existing website structure, without access to live user data, and within a tight project timeline.

Research

Usability testing the current experience

I conducted usability tests on the live site. Participants located ticket options, selected a date and time, chose two adult tickets, and attempted guest checkout. Their experience surfaced six recurring pain points:

  • Unclear starting point between “Plan a Visit,” “Get Tickets,” and “Buy Non-Member Tickets.”
  • Multiple redirects and new tabs disrupted the flow.
  • Long pages made reviewing options difficult.
  • Date and time pickers were inconsistent and unintuitive.
  • Seasonal pricing, taxes, and totals were not visible early enough.
  • Buttons, checkboxes, and links did not consistently look interactive.

Design process

A guest-first task flow

I mapped a single-page flow that combines date, time, and ticket quantity selection. Clear progress feedback and a guest-first option reduce the number of decisions and keep users moving forward.

From sketches to prototype

I began with paper wireframes to explore layout and the placement of ticket options, date/time selectors, and navigation. In mid-fidelity Figma designs, I refined spacing, hierarchy, and button placement with feedback from peers and my instructor. The final high-fidelity prototype added branding, visual design, interactive components, and hover states to simulate the completed flow.

Results & iteration

I tested the redesigned prototype with three participants completing a guest purchase for two adults. Testing showed that seasonal pricing needed clearer explanation, taxes were expected earlier, clickable cards needed stronger affordances, and action colors needed more consistency.

In response, I standardized button colors and the Back/Next hierarchy, added seasonal-pricing explanation, introduced earlier tax and total previews, and strengthened hover states and other interactive cues. These changes reduced cognitive load and made the purchase flow clearer and more reassuring.

Reflection

This project reinforced how iterative testing reveals small friction points that can meaningfully affect a user’s confidence. Clear affordances, consistent interactions, and earlier price transparency improved the experience. Future work would include 5–8 additional moderated tests, mobile-specific flows, alternative payment options, and a WCAG AA accessibility audit for contrast and keyboard navigation.
